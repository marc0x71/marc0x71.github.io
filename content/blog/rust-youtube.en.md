Okay, I have a confession to make: I'm not the only one talking about Rust on YouTube. I know, shocking — but we're in good company: there are 41 other channels genuinely worth following. Go ahead and explore them, but let's make a deal: don't you dare unsubscribe from my channel, or I'll be offended! 😉

Learning Rust isn't easy. The language has a steep learning curve, unique concepts like ownership and the borrow checker, and a rapidly evolving ecosystem. One of the most valuable — and often underestimated — resources out there is YouTube: dozens of dedicated channels exist, ranging from tutorials for absolute beginners to advanced live coding sessions diving deep into the language internals.

This is the list I've built up over time, one channel at a time. I'm sharing it in the hope that it can be useful to anyone just starting out or looking to go deeper.

## Starting from scratch

If you're taking your first steps with Rust, the best approach is to start with channels that follow a structured path rather than scattered individual videos.

**[Let's Get Rusty](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCSp-OaMpsO8K0KkOqyBl7_w)** is probably the most recommended starting point: the channel follows *The Rust Programming Language Book* (the official manual) chapter by chapter, with clear explanations and a steady pace.

**[Doug Milford](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCmBgC0JN41HjyjAXfkdkp-Q)** offers a very systematic independent series, designed for people coming from other languages who want to understand every concept without taking anything for granted.

**[timClicks](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UClny6qj9Mv7uFo9XGUGYQBA)** is the channel of Tim McNamara, author of the book *Rust in Action*: his approach is practical and problem-solving oriented.

For those who prefer **content in Italian**, the three main references are: **[Carlo Milanesi](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCSMvJIxz5g8EZYYGIzsei3Q)** (author of *Beginning Rust* and of *Rust. Dalle basi del linguaggio alle tecniche avanzate*) — on his channel he focuses on Rust programming on Linux with a very hands-on approach, reimplementing classic Unix tools in Rust, which makes it particularly interesting for anyone who wants to see the language applied to real problems), **[Il Codice di Marco](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCY4hel_LJvh7iGHLFG1EV9g)** (my channel, where you'll find tutorials and in-depth content in Italian) and **[iDesoft Learn Italiano](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCOTPtobC5oml5wKc9WGzaJg)**, which has recently started a playlist on concurrency in Rust.

## Intermediate level: consolidating and specializing

Once you've got the basics down, it's time to pick a direction.

**For those who want to go deeper into concepts** without yet diving into internals, **[No Boilerplate](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCUMwY9iS8oMyWDYIe6_RmoA)** and **[Code to the Moon](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCjREVt2ZJU8ql-NC9Gu-TJw)** are excellent: short, dense, well-crafted videos. **[Oliver Jumpertz](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCAdUYGE2WMj7U57BKwg83rg)** covers similar ground but with a broader scope: Rust and system design, with an approachable and accessible style.

**For web backend**, the go-to is **[Jeremy Chone](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCiT_r1GD7JSftnbViKHcOtQ)**: he works primarily with Axum and demonstrates production-ready best practices.

**For game development with Bevy**, the leading channel is **[chris biscardi](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCiSIL42pQRpc-8JNiYDFyzQ)**, with content updated for each new version of the framework. **[Brooks Builds](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCT1-XRVnJA-wws2bfbLbFcQ)** is an all-around live coding channel: topics range from building a shell in Rust to CLI tools, full-stack projects and much more. The real strength is watching the development process unfold in real time, mistakes included — very useful for understanding how an intermediate-to-advanced programmer thinks.

**For CLI and TUI**, **[Orhun Parmaksız](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCD9CsJAoFiFjFWaw-S2e6Hg)** is an active maintainer of several open source tools written in Rust (including `gitui` and `ratatui`): his videos show real development on real projects. **[developerlife.com](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCMcsxfCwzwDevc3NRqFgfEg)** covers both Ratatui and Bevy with well-structured tutorials.

**For embedded**, **[DigiKey](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCclJCqMDAkyVGSm5oFOTXIQ)** is the most comprehensive channel: it covers Rust on microcontrollers with hands-on examples using real hardware.

## Advanced level: deep diving into internals

These channels are not for those taking their first steps, but they represent some of the most technically valuable Rust resources available on YouTube.

**[Jon Gjengset](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UC_iD0xppBwwsrM9DegC5cQQ)** is the most cited name in the advanced community. His *Crust of Rust* series tackles the internal mechanisms of the standard library and the language in detail: sessions are long (often 2–3 hours) but packed with content. He is also the author of *Rust for Rustaceans: Idiomatic Programming for Experienced Developers* (No Starch Press, 2021): if the Rust Book is the starting point, this is the essential next step for anyone who wants to use Rust professionally. It covers advanced ownership, the trait system, async/await, unsafe code, procedural macros and much more — everything the Rust Book intentionally leaves out.

**[fasterthanlime](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCs4fQRyl1TJvoeOdekW6lYA)** — real name Amos Wenger — produces articles and videos that dig deep into async, linking, compilation and non-obvious language behaviors. Analytical and very thorough.

**[Logan Smith](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCnHX5FjwtQpxkCGziuh4NJA)** focuses on the advanced type system, lifetimes and ownership: short videos but very high conceptual density.

**[Ryan Levick](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCpeX4D-ArTrsqvhLapAHprQ)** has worked on Rust at Microsoft and his content covers systems programming, C++ interoperability and using Rust in enterprise contexts.

**[Core Dumped](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCGKEMK3s-ZPbjVOIuAV8clQ)** covers operating systems, internals and systems programming with a rigorous and well-explained approach.

## Official channels and conferences

To stay up to date on where the language is heading, conference channels are a precious resource: they gather talks from core team members, contributors and practitioners.

**[EuroRust](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCR3gXcme1HMK7_TrUaNZOqw)** and **[Rust Nation UK](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCLksRXfBiEITZMUo2ssjSdA)** are the two most important European conferences. **[RustLab Conference](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCBdn2g49kLEyjlssavwbxdQ)** is the Italian Rust conference: talks are partly in Italian, partly in English.

**[Rust Foundation](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UC0jzvznwtnsdXYIp415oC9g)** and **[Rust Programming Language](http://www.youtube.com/channel/UCaYhcUwRBNscFNUKTjgPFiA)** are the official channels: webinars, announcements and content from the project's governance.
